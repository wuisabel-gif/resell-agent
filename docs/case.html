The case

A wardrobe accumulates value it rarely realises.

The bag out of rotation, the barely-worn coat, the watch kept in a drawer, each is worth real money to the right buyer. Selling it well is its own discipline.

What selling well takes

Three things stand between a piece and its price

The willingness to sell was never the problem. The work is in the doing, and it is the same three steps every time.

The price

The honest market figure, drawn from real comparables, not a lowball, not a guess. Given as a range you set to taste, never false precision.

The words

A listing that reads considered rather than eager, in the voice each platform rewards, keyword-dense on eBay, warmer on Poshmark.

The placement

On eBay and Poshmark, where the buyers for these pieces are already looking, with category and item specifics resolved for you.

The cost of waiting

Value kept is value quietly lost

A piece left in a closet is money that has stopped working. Styles move on, condition softens, and the space it occupies is space you pay for. The best moment to sell was when you stopped reaching for it; the next best is before another season turns.

Where the value hides

The pieces that hold their price

Some categories resell far better than others. These are the ones worth photographing first.

Designer handbags

The strongest resale of all. A recognised house in good condition returns most of its retail, and the discontinued can ask more.

Outerwear

Coats and jackets photograph clearly and price with confidence; a good wool or a technical shell holds across seasons.

Footwear

Sought-after heels, boots, and sneakers move quickly once the size and the condition are stated plainly.

Denim & contemporary

The everyday middle of the market: high volume, quick sales, where a clean, correct listing wins the click.

Jewellery & watches

Small, high in value, and forgiving of trend. Authentication matters most here, so the tool flags rather than claims.

Occasion pieces

The worn-once dress, the event coat, bought at a premium and sold on with much of that premium intact.

The discipline, automated

By hand, or in a photograph

By hand

  • Trawl sold listings to guess a price
  • Write a title that buyers actually search
  • Pick the right category from thousands
  • Fill in every item specific by hand
  • Rewrite it all again for the next platform

In a photograph

  • A price range from real comparables
  • A keyword-dense title, written for you
  • Category and specifics resolved automatically
  • The house read or inferred, flagged to verify
  • eBay and Poshmark copy, both in one pass
Everything at once

One photograph, not one at a time

The real tedium was never the selling. It is the marking up: a title here, a category there, the same fields typed again for the next piece, and again for the one after that. So it does not ask you to. Point the camera at the whole look, or the whole pile on the bed, and it finds each sellable piece and drafts them together, priced and written, in a single pass.
